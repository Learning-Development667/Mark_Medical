# Daybook roadmap

The list of what is still to do, in the order to do it, kept in the repository so it is never lost in a chat.
Update it as things are finished: move the item to "Done", with the version number. Started 29 September 2026 at v71.
A simplified live copy with tick boxes is published at https://claude.ai/artifact/PCi6gEEHuHR9idt9fPbwks (private to Mark, pinned in his Claude sidebar); republish it whenever this file changes.

How to read it: each item says what it is, why it matters, who does what (Mark, or Claude in the code), and what "done" looks like.
The order follows one idea: what Mark uses every day comes first, then the loose ends and the new address, then the foundations
other people's data would sit on (with the condition profiles, which shape every screen after them), then the look, then the
developer review, then the company and the app stores, which are the slowest and hardest to undo, then more condition profiles.
New ideas go into this file in the right place before they are built, so the order stays true.

Where it stands (Claude's best guess, 4 October 2026, at v103): about 60 percent of the way to both stores, roughly 30 build days
left to submission, which at the pace so far is six to eight weeks of calendar time, with the company and Apple paperwork waiting
in parallel. Every item in Stage 7 adds a few days each on top. Revisit this line when a stage closes.

Decisions already made, so they are not reopened:
- Cost is no longer the constraint. The £79 a year Apple Developer Programme and small running costs are accepted (Mark, 28 September 2026). The free tiers stay because they work, not because they must.
- Legal matters (trademark, company) wait until the app is polished. The name is **My Medical Daybook** in full, Daybook inside the app (Mark, 1 October 2026, v89): the bare word Daybook belongs to a journal app, the full phrase does not, and the phrase says what the app is before anyone opens it.
- The data screens stay plain, with no background pictures behind the cards and charts. The sign-in screen has the watercolour and that is where it stays.
- The app never names one AI assistant in its wording. "Daybook's AI service" means the bridge calling the Anthropic API with Mark's own key and one-off credit; nobody using the app needs anything of their own.

## Stage 6: the company and the app stores
Daybook is one web app, so both stores get the same code. Android users can install it from Chrome today with reminders and recordings working; only the health feed and the store listing are platform work.
18. **The admin with long waits: company, D-U-N-S and ICO** (new item, gathered from the notes; can start any time, because it is mostly waiting). Apple wants health apps from a legal entity: either a small limited company (Mark 1 Apps Ltd, about £50 to set up at Companies House) or a charity, hospice or NHS team publishing under its own account. A company then needs a D-U-N-S number (free, about two weeks) to enrol as an organisation. Register with the ICO (about £52 a year) before any household outside the family uses it. Mark. Done when the company and its D-U-N-S number exist and the ICO registration is in place.
19. **Join the Apple Developer Programme** (was 18). About £79 a year, as the company once item 18 is done (or as Mark himself for TestFlight only, converted later). Mark's one-off steps: enrol, accept the agreements in App Store Connect, make an App Store Connect API key and save it as a repository secret. Done when GitHub can upload a build.
20. **Build the native shells, with Siri** (was 19, plus the Siri entry from Parked). iPhone: a thin app around the same web code, with Apple Health read directly (no Health Auto Export needed), proper notifications, and Siri actions through App Intents ("Log a dose", "Read this medicine", "Summarise this letter"), using Apple Intelligence on the phone or Apple's Private Cloud Compute as the reader and summariser beside or instead of the Anthropic credit. Android: a Trusted Web Activity wrapper and a Health Connect feed. Both built on GitHub Actions on hosted machines, no Mac needed. Claude. Done when each shell runs the app from the same repository with no second copy of the code, and "Hey Siri, log a dose" works on Mark's phone.
21. **TestFlight and Play internal testing, then submission** (was 20). Mark and Shelley first, then a small group, then submission to both stores with a demo login for the reviewers, the privacy answers and the website's privacy and support pages. Google Play: a one-off $25 developer account; Google leans the same way as Apple on health apps coming from an organisation, but less strictly. Done when both stores list Daybook.
22. **Trade mark application for "My Medical Daybook"** (was in Parked). About £170 for one class in the UK, once the app is going to the stores, after an IPO register search in classes 9, 42 and 44. Mark. Copyright and the LICENSE already cover the code, the artwork and the strapline.

## Stage 7: more condition profiles (Mark, 4 October 2026)
The mechanism and Crohn's come in item 9; these follow one at a time, each a few days with its sources checked, not a rebuild. Each needs the tiles, the check-in questions, the Notes topics and thresholds with sources, demo data, a test file, and a line in the privacy pages if anything new is stored. The demo gains a profile switcher so each audience sees its own Today.
23. **Menopause.** Hot flushes as a counted tile, sleep, mood, periods and how heavy, HRT as the medicines, a symptoms check-in. A large audience badly served by apps that are really period trackers.
24. **Long-term pain, fibromyalgia, ME.** Pain, energy, sleep, what was managed, a pacing view; the check-ins already ask most of it.
25. **Heart failure and kidney disease.** Weight every morning, blood pressure, fluid in and out, breathing: the diary the nurses hand out on paper.
26. **COPD and asthma.** Breathing, oxygen, a peak flow reading (new), rescue inhaler use as a when-needed medicine.
27. **Diabetes.** A glucose reading (new); everything else is there.
28. **Migraine and epilepsy.** An attack or seizure log with triggers; Trends and Notes carry the value.
29. **Dementia and frailty, carer-led.** The carer's view becomes the main check-in: mood, eating, drinking, falls, who visited.
30. **Recovery after surgery.** A short-lived profile: wound, pain, mobility, the exercises, the follow-up questions.
