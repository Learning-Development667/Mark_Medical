# Daybook

Private, shared health tracker for Mark and Shelley. Hosted on GitHub Pages from `main`, root.
Live: https://learning-development667.github.io/Mark_Medical/

The full name is **My Medical Daybook** (since v89, Mark's decision of 1 October 2026 after a day of name screening found Daybook on its own taken by a journal app and every short health word crowded); inside the app it is simply **Daybook**, because once you are in, it is obviously yours. Named Daybook since v48 (it was Care Log until then; the repository, the Pages path and older notes below keep the old name). Strapline, on the sign-in screen and for any store listing: "Your companion between appointments."
- The full name appears only on the cover: the opener (`.opener-pre` "My Medical" stacked above the big `.opener-brand` "Daybook", and its aria-label), the four sign-in screens (`#signin`, `#signup`, `#nohousehold`, `#noconfig`: `.brand-pre` above the brand in the card), the static `<title>` and the bare `setBrand()` title, `manifest.json` `name` (the store listing), `README.md`, `LICENSE`, and the pitch and user guide when they are written. It is never written on one line in the app's own type; it is always "My Medical" small and tracked over "Daybook" large, so it fits a 320px card.
- Daybook alone is everywhere inside: the topbar (`setBrand()`), `document.title` per page, `manifest.json` `short_name` and the Home Screen title (`apple-mobile-web-app-title`), the PDF footer, the share title, the first line of the copied notes, every button and hint ("Explain with Daybook Assistant", "Create my own Daybook"), the Settings App card. Do not add "My Medical" to any of those.
- The pasted-document header `=== CARE LOG DOCUMENT ===` is a data format and deliberately unchanged.

Speech to text (since v48): `speakButton(textarea)` adds a big "Tap to speak" button under a text box, using the browser's own speech recognition (`SpeechRecognition`/`webkitSpeechRecognition`: Apple's on iPhone, Google's in Chrome; nothing of ours in between, no key, no cost). Words appear in the box as recognised and can be edited like typed text; it turns warm and reads "Listening, tap to stop" while active. Wired under the Note sheet, the Question sheet, every check-in text step. Not rendered where the browser has no support, where the keyboard's own microphone still works.

## Standards
- Body text minimum 17px. Tap targets minimum 48px (44px is the hard floor for secondary controls). The user may be tired or drowsy: clarity beats density.
- Palette: ink #18262B, surface #FFFFFF, background #EDF1F2, accent teal #1E5F74, warm terracotta #C2703D (emphasis only), alert red #A4262C (38.0°C and above), amber #9A5B00 (37.5 to 37.9°C and waits), green #2E6B45 (done). Full dark mode via prefers-color-scheme. Never loud.
- Service worker: network-first ALWAYS. skipWaiting() on install, delete ALL caches on activate, network-first on fetch, never cache-first.
- Cache busting: `?v=VERSION` on `scripts.js` and `styles.css` in `index.html`, and the `APP_VERSION` constant in `scripts.js`. Bump on every commit.
- Run `node --check` on every JS file before committing.
- UK English throughout. No em dashes anywhere in UI copy.
- No health data in the repo, ever. Only code.
- Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 level AA, checked in v53 with axe-core (the App card under More > Settings says so, with the date) on every screen and sheet in both colour schemes (zero violations) plus hand checks for what a scanner cannot judge. Keep it that way: every sheet is a real dialog (`openSheet()` makes `.skip-link, .topbar, #main, #tabs` inert, moves focus to the title, or the first field on wide screens, and `closeSheet()` returns focus to whatever opened it; Escape closes, and `confirmSheet()` treats any close as Cancel); one keyboard focus ring for everything (`:focus-visible`, teal, never shown on tap); a skip link to `#main`; the toast is `role="status"`; the active tab carries `aria-current="page"`; `document.title` names the screen; every chart canvas gets `role="img"` and an `aria-label` built in `makeChart()` from the card's heading, the plotted range and its note; the diary donut is `aria-hidden` because its legend carries the numbers; an `aria-label` on a plain `div` or `span` needs a role (`group`, `img`); no tap target under 24px (the app's own floor is 44px) and nothing overflows at 320px wide, so the medicine purpose pill wraps, the two custom-range dates wrap, the five range buttons wrap under 360px and the calendar cells drop their 48px minimum there; in dark mode `--teal` is #5FB0C8 and `--teal-light` #142B34 (both chosen for 4.5:1), and `--teal-dark` is a button colour there, not a text colour (`.note-who`, `.speakbtn`, `.diary-macros`, `.macro-total`, `.painbtn.is-teal` switch to `--teal` in dark). AAA is not claimed: 1.4.6 enhanced contrast and 2.4.13 focus appearance are not met throughout.
- Design tokens live at the top of `styles.css` and are used everywhere; no hex values scattered through the code (the only exception is `PDF_TEAL` in `scripts.js`, because PDFs are always on white paper). Colour: `--surface`, `--surface-raised`, `--surface-sunken`, `--border-subtle`, `--text-primary/secondary/muted`, `--teal/-dark/-light`, `--warm/-dark/-light` (terracotta #C2703D, the one warm accent: attention and human warmth, used sparingly), `--success`, `--warning`, `--danger` (thresholds unchanged), `--cat-*` timeline category colours, `--sleep-deep/-core/-rem` (a single-hue ramp for the sleep chart, deepest to lightest) and `--mood-1..5` for the calendar. Every text token is at least 4.5:1 on the page, a card and the sunken surface, in both colour schemes.

## Data model (Firestore)
Since v75 every collection below sits inside a household: `households/{hid}/entries/...`, `households/{hid}/medicines/...` and so on (see Households). The paths in this section are written without that prefix; in the app they are reached only through `hcol(name, ...)` and `hdoc(name, ...ids)`, which prepend `households/{state.household}`, and on the bridge through `hp(hid, path)`.
- `entries/{autoId}`: { day "YYYY-MM-DD", at Timestamp, type "med"|"temp"|"drink"|"food"|"weight"|"vitals"|"note"|"sleep", medId?, medName?, dose?, value? (number), heartRate? (number, bpm), systolic? (number, mmHg), diastolic? (number, mmHg), oxygen? (number, %), note?, amount? (food: "A few mouthfuls"|"About half"|"Most of it"|"All of it"), detail? (food: what went with it), deep?/rem?/core?/awake? (sleep stages, minutes), addedBy, createdAt }
  - Daily check-ins: `entries/{YYYY-MM-DD}_{morning|evening}` (deterministic id, so a day and slot can only ever have one document and reopening edits it): { type "checkin", slot, day, at, addedBy, createdAt, updatedAt, then one field per answer. Morning: sleep (1 to 10), sleepHours (number), pain, mood, symptoms (text), lookingForward (text). Evening: pain, mood, worstPain, sickness, appetite, energy, symptoms, settled, goodThing }. A skipped slider is stored as `null`, skipped text as `""`, never 0. Questions are defined in `CHECKIN_QUESTIONS`; the flow (`openCheckin`) is one question per screen with a progress bar, Back and Skip on every step, a Today/Yesterday picker on the first step, and a review screen before saving. Saving mirrors mood to `days/{day}.mood` (1 to 5, `ceil(mood/2)`) and the evening `goodThing` to `days/{day}.good`, so the Chemo calendar faces, Notes for the team and the low-mood flag keep working; the Chemo day sheet no longer edits mood or "one good thing". Home shows both slots with their state (Due now before 15:00 for morning, after for evening; Done HH:MM; Missed; Later today), and since v54 the Question for the team row sits under them in the same list, so the three align.
  - Pain readings: `entries/{autoId}` { type "pain", value 1 to 10, note? } from the Pain tile in Today's quick-add grid (`openAdd('pain')`, since v54; until then a "Log pain now" button above the grid, dropped because the check-ins already ask twice a day and the between-times reading deserved a tile, not a banner). The Trends Pain chart draws the line from check-in `pain` scores and shows these as hollow points; the Mood chart uses check-in `mood`. Notes for the team flags pain 5 to 6 amber and 7+ red (check-in or reading), worstPain 7+, sickness 6+, appetite or energy 3 and under, and lists the check-in text answers as notes.
  - Sleep entries: `value` is minutes asleep for the night before, logged against the morning (`day` is the wake-up date). Since 2026-09-24 they arrive automatically from Apple Health through the bridge as `entries/{day}_sleep` (`addedBy: "Apple Health"`, see Bridge); the Sleep sheet is for corrections and missed nights. If the selected morning already has a sleep entry the sheet opens it for editing (`editId`, `updateEntry()`) instead of adding a second one. The four stage fields (awake, rem, core, deep, in Apple's order) are always shown, and `bedAt`/`wokeAt` ("HH:MM" strings) record the night's window, with the entry's `at` set to the wake time. "Woke at" fills itself in from bedtime plus time asleep plus any awake minutes until it is typed in by hand. Shown on the Today timeline, as a Latest tile and a bar chart in Trends, in the readings line of Notes for the team, and flagged there as a short night under 5 hours.
  - Food entries: `note` is the meal name, `detail` the optional "what is in it" text. The Food diary (Trends > Reports) lists food entries day by day with each day's drinks total, over 7 to 90 days or a custom range (see Report ranges below), and has a Print button (print stylesheet in `styles.css`).
  - Calorie and macro estimates (since v42, optional): gated everywhere on `profile/main.detailedNutrition` (bool, account-wide, the toggle under More > Settings > Food, `#settings-nutrition`, `syncSettings()`). Off by default and off means nothing runs, nothing is stored and the app is exactly as it was. On, a food entry may carry `portions?: { [componentKey]: { size "S"|"M"|"L", override: null | { kcal, prot, carb, fat, per "portion"|"100g", grams } } }`, keyed per component by `macroComponentKey()` (`c:` plus the CoFID/ADDED_FOODS food code, or `u:` plus the typed phrase for something the table has nothing for). Nothing else about macros is stored; `entryMacros(index, entry)` sits beside `entryNutrition()` and recomputes on render from the same `matches` (the tag logic is untouched): grams = the component's food group's Small/Medium/Large default (`portionGroupFor()`, "typical portion, estimate", Medium by default) times `FOOD_AMOUNT_SCALE` for the entry's existing "how much" answer (0.25/0.5/0.75/1), then the row's per 100 g figures. A manual "Enter from the packet" override always wins (per portion is used as typed; per 100 g is scaled by its own `grams`); next comes a food in `profile/main.customFoods` (`[{ name, kcal, prot, carb, fat, addedBy }]` per 100 g, written by "Save to my foods" in the override panel, matched by the typed name, `customFoodByName()`; `ADDED_FOODS` in the code stays separate and curated); then the table row. A component with none of those is left out and counted ("not counted", `unmatchedChunks()` catches an unknown ingredient beside known ones, which the tag matcher would otherwise drop silently). Four render sites, all gated: the Food sheet (behind the Estimate line since v54: one row per component with S/M/L buttons showing the grams, the override panel, a totals line; a saved meal chosen there restores its own `meals/{id}.portions`), Today's timeline (`entrySub()`, "about N kcal, N g protein"; `ensureFoodIndexForToday()` fetches the table in the background and redraws once), the Food diary and its PDF/print (`diaryRow()` line per entry, `.diary-macros` per day, `dayMacros()`), and the per-day readings line of Notes for the team (`dayReadings()`, so screen, PDF and the Send/Copy text all carry it). Estimates, not medical advice, and the UI says so.
  - Editing an entry in place (since v37): food, drink, weight and note entries can be edited, not just deleted, via the same "..." entry menu (`entryOptions()`, `EDITABLE_ENTRY_TYPES`) reopening `openAdd(type, entry)` pre-filled with the entry's own values and date/time, saving with `updateEntry()` instead of creating a new one. Food diary rows (`diaryRow()`) get their own "..." menu too, so a wrong "how much" or a missing "what's in it" can be fixed from the diary itself, not just from Today's timeline; `renderFoodDiary()` re-runs after a save or delete made from there so the diary reflects the change immediately, since it fetches its own range with `loadEntriesFrom()` rather than following the live listener Today uses. Since v105 temperature and vitals readings edit too (Mark, from a hospital bed, expected a view and an edit where the menu only offered Delete): the "..." menu is titled by the entry's kind (`ENTRY_SHEET_TITLES`: Vitals, Temperature, Medicine and so on, no longer "Entry"), and for a reading lists one line per value (`readingRows()`, a `.readings` definition list: Heart rate 88 bpm, Blood pressure 122/82 mmHg, Oxygen 98%, coloured amber or red with the Notes for the team thresholds), then **Edit**, which opens the Vitals sheet as "Edit vitals" or "Edit temperature" with only that entry's boxes, filled in (the other blocks hidden; weight keeps its own edit sheet), the note, date and time, and **Save changes**. A cleared box is saved as `null` so it really goes; an edit with every box empty is refused with "use Delete"; a changed date moves the reading (the update now sets `day` when the sheet has a Date box). Tested in `pw/v105.js` (the view and its levels, Edit vitals and Edit temperature filled in with the other boxes hidden, a cleared heart rate, one entry kept, the empty edit refused, a temperature moved to yesterday and shown amber there, axe, 320px dark). Medicines, sleep, sessions and check-ins have their own edit routes or stay delete-and-re-add.
  - Nutrition tags (since v29): `data/cofid.json` is the official UK food table (CoFID 2021, McCance and Widdowson, Open Government Licence; 2,887 foods, per 100 g: kcal, prot, fat, sat, carb, sugar, starch, fibre (AOAC), nsp, salt unused), built and committed by `.github/workflows/build-cofid.yml` (`tools/build_cofid.py`, run by hand from the Actions tab; the gov.uk download only works from a runner). The app fetches it lazily (`loadFoodTable()`, cached in `foodIndex`) when the Food sheet or Food diary first needs it. `matchFoodText()` splits an entry's `note` plus `detail` on commas and "and/with/on", drops quantities and stop words, and looks each phrase up: the curated `FOOD_ALIASES` first (everyday words to table names, including composites such as "roast dinner", "fajitas", "full English", "homity pie"), then the table's own head words (`buildFoodIndex()`, best plain cooked form picked by `pickRepresentative()`), then two-word windows and single words, with a one-letter typo fallback for longer words. Cooking words never match on their own. `foodTags()` gives each matched food label-style tags per 100 g: Protein (10 g or 20% of energy with 5 g), Fibre (over 3 g or 1.5 g per 100 kcal), Wholegrain (starchy group plus a wholegrain word in the name), Fruit and veg (groups D and F except potatoes, juices), Dairy (group B except desserts), Starchy carbs, Good fats (nuts, oily fish, avocado, mostly unsaturated), High sugar (over 22.5 g, not fruit or veg), High fat (over 17.5 g), High sat fat (over 5 g). Small-amount groups (sauces, sugars and spreads, herbs, fats and oils) earn no tags. An entry's tags are the union of its components' tags in `FOOD_TAG_ORDER`. The diary shows the tags as chips coloured by group (`.tag.is-veg/-protein/-carb`, `NUTRI_GROUP` in the JS, since v41): Fruit and veg, Protein plus Good fats, and Fibre/Wholegrain/Starchy carbs each get their own hue (`--nutri-veg/-protein/-carb`, chosen and checked against colour-blindness with the data-viz palette validator, both colour schemes); Dairy stays a plain neutral chip and the "High ..." ones reuse the app's existing warm accent, since that is a status signal, not a fourth identity colour, kept deliberately to three so they stay tellable apart. Each day with any tags also gets a small donut chart of that day's colour mix (`donutGradient()`, a CSS conic-gradient) with a coloured-swatch legend beside it, above the existing per-day tag count line, a per-day tag count line, "Not recognised: ..." for phrases nothing matched (screen only), and an overview (`#food-overview`, days with each tag out of the days shown). The PDF has the same overview line, the same donut per day (drawn as a solid pie of triangle wedges with a white circle punched over the middle for the hole, `wedge()`/`donut()` in `buildPdfBlob()`) and a two-column table per day (`{ kind: 'table', head, rows }`) whose Nutrition cells carry the same per-tag colours, wrapped word by word (`wrapTags()`) since jsPDF has no built-in multi-colour text run. The Food sheet nudges once when a name with no detail matches nothing ("picky lunch"): a shaded box explains it will still save either way, with its own "Save without tags" button, never a silent second tap. Nothing about the tags is stored; they are recomputed on render, so improving `FOOD_ALIASES` improves every past entry. Not medical advice, and the diary says so.
  - `ADDED_FOODS` in `scripts.js` (next to `buildFoodIndex()`) holds branded products the official table was never going to have, added by hand from a photo of the product's own label, per 100 g, in the same shape as a table row (`g: null`, since CoFID's group codes don't apply). Each carries its own `aliases`, which take priority over a generic table guess for the same words. First entry: Whey protein powder, Gold Standard, strawberry (real label figures: 79 g protein, 3.3 g sugar, no fibre per 100 g; a stand-in like the table's "Build up" shake would have wrongly tagged it "High sugar"). Its generic aliases (`whey protein`, `protein powder`, `whey`) mean any whey product gets this profile until a closer match is added; close enough for whey specifically, since brands don't vary hugely, but worth another one added if a very different product turns up.
  - Questions for the team (since v45): `entries/{autoId}` { type "question", note (the question), answered (bool), addedBy, day, at } from the "Question for the team" row on Today (`#question-now`, since v54 the third row of the check-in list, same shape as the two check-ins, with a status line from `renderQuestionRow()`: "2 waiting for the next appointment, tap to add another" or "Nothing waiting, tap to add one"; `watchQuestions()` keeps `state.questions` live for it, the demo reads `recentEntries`; `openAdd('question')`, so it edits in place like a note). Shown on the timeline with a teal bar; the "..." menu offers Mark as answered / Reopen. Open questions from any date (not just the range) head the Notes for the team report, numbered, with a Mark as answered button there too; questions marked answered inside the range are counted in one muted line. `loadQuestions()` fetches them with a single `where('type', '==', 'question')` query.
  - Notes for the team (Trends > Reports), rebuilt in v45 and v46 to fit a ten-minute appointment. Four sections, on screen, in the PDF and in the Send/Copy text: (1) Questions for the team (in the PDF each has a box to tick, "Asked by Mark, 24 Sep" and, since v55, three faint ruled lines under it to write the answer on in clinic; the print stylesheet leaves the same room under each `.question` card); (2) Summary (`summaryRows()`): a few plain sentences, worst first, not statistics. Only topics worth checking or mentioning get a line of their own ("Pain has increased, from about 4/10 in the first half of the period to 6/10 in the second; 5 or more on 7 days, worst 8/10 on 23 Sep", "High temperature (38.0 or over) on 2 days of the 12 with a reading, peaking at 38.7 °C on 24 Sep"); a trend compares the second half of the period with the first when both halves have at least three scores (pain, sickness, appetite, energy). - `profile/main.programme.items`: [{ id, name, section "exercise"|"physio", kind "reps"|"seconds"|"minutes"|"sets"|"lengths"|"distance"|"do", amount (reps, seconds, minutes, reps per set, lengths, a distance in `dunit`, or 1 for "do"), sets?, pool? (metres, lengths only, 25 by default), dunit? ("km"|"m", distance only), time? (target minutes, lengths and distance), match? ("walk"|"swim"|"run"|"cycle"|"any", since v73), days? ([getDay() numbers], none or empty meaning every day), note?, since? "YYYY-MM-DD" }] (`PROGRAMME_KINDS`, `itemTarget()` prints "20 reps", "1 minute", "3 sets of 20", "30 × 25 m in 30 minutes", "1.5 km", "once"; distances read as people say them, `fmtMetres()`: 750 m, 1.2 km). An account with no `programme` gets `defaultProgrammeItems()`: Press-ups, Sit-ups, Plank, Squats with the old `exerciseGoals` amounts and the old ids, so every day already ticked still counts. `since` is set on anything added in the app: before that day the item is not due (`itemDue()`), so adding an exercise never turns past days into missed ones.
- Ticking from Apple Health (since v73): an item with `match` is ticked by that day's workouts of that activity (`workoutActivity()` reads the workout's name: walk or hike, swim, run or jog, cycle or bike; "any" takes every workout), added together, once they reach the target (`itemHealth()`: minutes for a minutes item, metres for lengths and distance, any matching workout for the rest). The row shows "Apple Health: 35 min · 800 m", or "Apple Health so far: 18 of 20 min" / "400 m of 750 m" short of it. `isItemDone()` is the one test everywhere (rows, `dayStatus()`, the streak): ticked by hand, or reached through Health and not unticked by hand; unticking a Health tick writes `exercise/{day}.off[id] = true` and ticking again clears it. The item sheet's "Tick it from Apple Health" chips (Off, Walk, Swim, Run, Cycle, Any workout) follow the typed name until chosen by hand ("Evening swim" picks Swim).
- The tab (`renderExercise()`): a **From Apple Health** card when the day has `workouts` (name, "18 min · 1.1 km · 70 kcal · 08:40" or "35 min · 800 m", then "Counts towards Swim" in green when a matching item is due that day, otherwise **Add to programme**, which opens the item sheet pre-filled by `itemFromWorkout()`: a swim as a distance in metres, anything else as minutes, with matching on); **Daily exercises**, one `.goal` row per item due that day (name, note, target; tap to tick, `toggleGoal(day, id)` writes `exercise/{day}.done[id]`), then "Not today: Walk (Mon, Wed, Fri)" for the rest (`.ex-not-today`); **Physio plan**, the same for `section: "physio"`, headed by the plan's details ("From Community physio, given 17 Sept 2026. Twice a day. Stop if pain goes above 5 out of 10.", `profile/main.physio`), hidden when there is neither; **Edit programme**. The streak counts days on which everything due was ticked, passing over rest days with nothing due (`dayStatus()`: done, rest or missed; `exerciseStreak()`).
- Programme sheet (`openProgrammeSheet()`): the items by section with move up and down, Edit and Remove (inline Yes, remove / Keep, never a second sheet), Add exercise, Add physio exercise, **Add from a photo or PDF of the plan** (only with the bridge, `explainAvailable()`), Physio plan details (`openPhysioDetailsSheet()`: who gave it, when, their instructions with Tap to speak), Done. Every change is saved as it is made (`saveProgramme(items, physio?)`, a merge on `profile/main`; in the preview it edits `state.profile`). The item sheet (`openItemSheet(item, isNew, after)`): name, Exercise or Physio, how it is counted (chips, the amount label follows), amount and sets, Every day or the day chips, a note.
- From a photo, PDF or Word file (`importPlanPhotos()`, "Add from a photo or PDF of the plan", since v70 for files, so an emailed plan works too): the files go through the document screen's own `processFiles()` (photos and PDF pages become page images, a `.docx` or `.txt` becomes text), at most `PLAN_MAX_PAGES` (8, the bridge's limit), posted to the bridge as `{ kind: "programme", pages }` or `{ kind: "programme", text }` (`EXPLAIN_SYSTEM.programme` asks for JSON only: from, given, physio, notes, items with name, kind, amount, sets, days, note; counts against the same daily cap, nothing kept). `parsePlanReply()` tolerates a code fence and prose around the JSON. The review sheet (`openPlanReviewSheet()`) lists what was read with a tick box each (all ticked), Daily exercises or Physio plan chips (Physio when the plan says so), the header for checking, and **Add the ticked ones**; a physio plan's header fills `profile/main.physio` for anything missing. Nothing is added without that tap.

## Keyboard and Vitals boxes (v93 and v94, 5 October 2026)
Two things Mark hit from a hospital bed.
- **The keyboard covered the check-in text box.** iPhone Safari lays the on-screen keyboard over the page instead of shrinking it, so the bottom-anchored sheet sat behind it and answers were typed blind. `keepSheetAboveKeyboard()` (next to the Escape handler) follows `window.visualViewport` (resize and scroll) and sets `--kb` (the keyboard's height: `innerHeight - vv.height - vv.offsetTop`) and `--vvh` (the visible height) on `#sheet`; `.sheet-panel` sits at `bottom: var(--kb, 0px)` with `max-height: min(92vh, calc(var(--vvh, 100vh) - 16px))`, and a focused text field inside the sheet is scrolled to its middle 320 ms after focus, once the keyboard has risen. The viewport meta carries `interactive-widget=resizes-content`, so Chrome on Android shrinks the page instead and `--kb` stays 0 there. Applies to every sheet, not just the check-ins.
- **Daily weights not saving.** Mark weighed himself daily but Trends held two readings in a week while oxygen had many. The save path is correct (tested three ways in `pw/weight.js`); the likely cause was the Vitals sheet showing the last weight inside the weight box as a grey placeholder ("78.6"), which reads as a value already entered, so Save went with the box empty. Every Vitals box is now empty when the sheet opens (no placeholder numbers at all, the grey "0"s in heart rate, blood pressure and oxygen went too), and the last temperature and weight show as a line under their boxes ("Last: 80.6 kg, 3 Oct 09:11."). Keep it that way: never put a plausible reading in a placeholder.
- **v94, the real cause found.** Mark checked the database itself: two weights, though he had typed one daily and seen "Vitals saved". The oxygen from the same sheets was all there, so the weight value was being lost between the box and the save. The boxes were `type="number"`, and when a number box cannot read what is in it (iPhone keypads and number boxes disagree over decimal points and stray characters) the browser reports it as empty, so the save went ahead without the weight and said nothing. Every Vitals box is now a text box with a number keypad (`inputmode` decimal or numeric, so the keypad is unchanged) read by `readNum(el)`: a comma or a full stop, spaces and a trailing unit are accepted ("80,6", "80.6 kg"); `{ empty }` when nothing was typed, `value: null` when something was typed that is not a number. `getData` checks every box against a believable range (temperature 30 to 45, heart rate 20 to 250, systolic 50 to 260, diastolic 20 to 160, oxygen 50 to 100, weight 20 to 300) and anything typed that cannot be read stops the whole save with a message naming the box ("Could not read the weight "eighty". Type just the number, like 80.6.") and focus on it; blood pressure needs both numbers. Nothing from the sheet is saved until every box reads. The saved message lists what was written (`savedVitalsText()`: "Vitals saved for 1 Oct: oxygen 96%, weight 80.6 kg"), so a missing reading shows at once. The sheet also gained a **Date** box beside Time (`dateIn`, no later than today, defaulting to the day Today shows), since it had always saved under whichever day Today had selected, which is today when opened from Trends' Log vitals; `openAdd()`'s save uses `dateIn` when a sheet sets one. The temperature box carries `class="input"` for the standard box style now it is a text box. Keep it that way: never let a typed reading be dropped without a message. Tested in `pw/v94.js` (the Date box from Trends and from Today, a past day filed correctly and shown in Trends, a future date refused, a comma, a unit, a word refused with nothing saved, the corrected save listing both readings, a weight under 20 refused, one blood pressure number refused, the keypads, no placeholder numbers, axe, no sideways scroll).

- Reminders on this phone (More > Settings > Reminders, `#settings-reminders`, `syncReminders()`/`setReminders()`): a Web Push subscription per phone, made with the public VAPID key in `config.js` (`window.DAYBOOK_PUSH.publicKey`, generated by `tools/vapid_keys.mjs`) and stored as `pushSubs/{id}` { endpoint, keys { p256dh, auth }, private? (bool, since v74), addedBy, uid, agent, addedAt } (`pushSubId()` hashes the endpoint). Off deletes the document and unsubscribes. The switch is disabled with a reason where push is not possible: the preview and the demo, a browser without push, an iPhone that has not added Daybook to the Home Screen (Safari only allows push for installed web apps), or notifications blocked in the phone's settings. `state.pushEnabled` reflects this phone.
- The bridge (`worker/`): a cron trigger every five minutes (`[triggers] crons` in `wrangler.toml`, free plan) runs `runReminders()`, household by household (`runHouseholdReminders()`): for each active scheduled medicine with `times`, in London time (`londonNow()`, `Europe/London`), a notification "Dalteparin 7,500 units is due" within 20 minutes after each time unless a dose for it was logged today from 60 minutes before the time onwards, and one nudge "Still to take: ..." 30 minutes after the time if still not logged; due `reminders` documents are sent ("Reminder: Creon 2 capsules") and marked `sent` (deleted a day later). `households/{hid}/bridge/reminderLog` { day, sent map } stops repeats across runs. Each notification goes to every `pushSubs` document in that household; a 404 or 410 from the push service deletes that subscription. `GET /push-test?key=<inbox key>` sends "Reminders are working on this phone." to every phone in that household, `GET /remind-now?key=<BRIDGE_KEY>` runs the check by hand for every household and returns what it did. Web Push is done with WebCrypto alone (`sendPush()`: VAPID ES256 token from the `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` secret, a JWK, `vapidHeader()`; payload encrypted per RFC 8291 aes128gcm, `encryptPayload()`; checked in the scratchpad against a simulated subscriber). The deploy workflow sets `VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY` on the worker when the repository secret exists; without it the bridge answers `skipped` and nothing is sent.
- Private notifications (since v74, `ROADMAP.md` item 4 at the time): per phone, on unless switched off, under the Reminders switch ("Keep medicine names off the lock screen", `#settings-private`, `syncPrivate()`, shown only while reminders are on). Stored as `pushSubs/{id}.private`; a phone saved before v74 has no field and counts as private. The bridge's `send()` gives a private phone the same notification with a nameless body (`payloadFor()`, `isPrivateSub()`): "A medicine is due", "A medicine is still to take", "Reminder: a medicine to take"; opening it lands on the Meds tab, which shows which one. Unit-tested in the scratchpad (`_test.payloadFor`).
- `sw.js` shows the notification (`push`: title, body, tag, icon) and a tap opens or focuses Daybook on `?tab=meds` (`notificationclick`); `enterApp()` honours `?tab=` and drops it from the address bar.
- Rules: `pushSubs` and `reminders` are family read/write (the bridge's service account bypasses rules). Notifications land a minute or two after the time at most, never to the second; Focus modes silence them like any other; clearing Safari's data means turning the switch on again.

## Bridge (Cloudflare Worker, `worker/`)
- Deployed as `care-log-bridge` on workers.dev by `.github/workflows/deploy-worker.yml` whenever `worker/**` changes on `main` (or by hand from the Actions tab). The job stops early with a notice, not a failure, until the four repository secrets exist: `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`, `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, `BRIDGE_KEY`, `FIREBASE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`.
- Writes to Firestore through the REST API with the Firebase service account (JWT signed with WebCrypto, token cached for the hour). A service account bypasses `firestore.rules`, so nothing in the rules changes for it.
- `POST /health`: the inbox for the Health Auto Export app (iOS, Premium/lifetime, background REST automation). Auth is the `X-Care-Log-Key` header carrying the household's inbox key (since v75, see Households; until then the single `BRIDGE_KEY`), and everything below is written inside that household. Metric `step_count` rows are summed per day into `exercise/{day}.steps` (merge, `addedBy: "Apple Health"`). Metric `sleep_analysis` rows become `entries/{day}_sleep` (deterministic id, `day` is the morning the sleep ended, `value` minutes asleep, `core`/`deep`/`rem`/`awake` minutes, `bedAt`/`wokeAt` "HH:MM" from the sleep start and end, `at` the sleep end, `addedBy: "Apple Health"`, `source: "health-auto-export"`), so re-sends update the same document. `data.workouts` (sent when Workouts is ticked in the automation) become `exercise/{day}.workouts`, one map per workout { name, minutes (from start and end, or a bare `duration`), km? (`distanceKm()`: `distance.qty` in km whatever the unit, miles, metres, yards or feet, since v73; before that a pool swim in metres would have read as kilometres), kcal? (`activeEnergyBurned.qty`), start? "HH:MM" }, the day's list replaced on each send. The last raw payload is kept at `households/{hid}/bridge/last` (trimmed to 20 KB) to check the exact field names after the first real send; the parser reads the sleep fields leniently (`asleep`/`totalSleep`, `sleepStart`/`inBedStart`, `sleepEnd`/`inBedEnd`).
- `POST /explain` is Daybook Assistant (since v60, see below). `GET /ping` is a health check; `/last` and `/push-test` take `?key=<inbox key>` and `/remind-now` `?key=<BRIDGE_KEY>`, see Medicine reminders. CORS is allowed only for the GitHub Pages origin, for the app's own future calls (nutrition lookups).
- Planned next on the bridge: `GET /food?barcode=` proxying Open Food Facts (keyless, 10 requests a minute, no CORS of its own), cached in a `foods` collection.

## Report ranges (since v42)
Notes for the team and the Food diary share one range control (`wireReportRange()`, `reportRange()`, `reportRangeLabel()`): the 7/14/30/90 day presets plus Custom, which shows two native date inputs (`#food-custom-from/-to`, `#notes-custom-from/-to`; To defaults to today, From to 14 days earlier, both capped at today). To may not be before From, and the range is capped at `CUSTOM_RANGE_MAX_DAYS` (180) with a short inline message (`.hint-warn`) that leaves the previous range showing. The header reads "From 21 Sep 2026 to 3 Oct 2026" for a custom range and keeps "Last 14 days, from ..." for presets; the PDF, print and the Send/Copy text use the same `from`/`to` (both inclusive; entries are fetched from `from` and filtered to `<= to`, `buildNotesReport(entries, from, to)`, `vitalsFlags(entries, from, to)`), and the Send/Copy text states the range in its first line. The last custom range lives in `state` (`foodRangeMode`, `foodCustomFrom/To`, the same for `notes`) for the session only, never in Firestore. Reason: appointments do not fall on neat 7 or 14 day boundaries.

## Households (since v75)
One locked folder per household. Everything a household records lives under `households/{hid}/...` (entries, medicines, documents and pages, recordings and parts, profile, days, exercise, meals, nutrition, pushSubs, reminders, plus `private` for its secrets and `bridge` for the bridge's logs). `households/{hid}` is { name, owner (uid), createdAt }. Who belongs is `households/{hid}/members/{uid}` { name, role "family"|"readonly"|"viewer", relation? "patient"|"carer"|"", addedBy, addedAt }, and `users/{uid}` { household } is the pointer a sign-in follows (own-read only). `firestore.rules` gates every path on that members record (`isMember(hid)`, `hasRole(hid, roles)`; roles keep the meanings below), so a member of one household cannot read a byte of another and a stranger nothing at all, enforced by the database, not the app. Nobody writes a household, a members record or a pointer from the app: the bridge (a service account) and the Actions-tab workflows do. A sign-in with no pointer, or one whose household has no members record for it, is refused with "This account is not in a Daybook household yet." One sign-in belongs to one household.
- App: `loadAccount()` reads `users/{uid}` then `households/{hid}/members/{uid}`, returns { name, role, relation, household } and caches it on the phone (a cache from before v75 names no household and is ignored); `enterApp()` sets `state.household`, cleared on sign-out. `hcol()`/`hdoc()` are the only route to data (the module comment above them says so); the shared demo uses the fixed household `demo` (`DEMO_HOUSEHOLD`, in the app and the bridge); the in-memory preview never touches Firestore.
- Bridge: `/health` takes the household's own inbox key (`healthKeys/{sha256(key)}` { household, label, createdAt }; keys are never stored, only hashes); reminders run household by household (`runHouseholdReminders()`), a phone only ever hears about its own; `/explain` finds the household from `users/{uid}.household` and requires a family members record, counting the daily cap in `households/{hid}/bridge/explainLog`; `/last` and `/push-test` take an inbox key, `/remind-now` the operator's `BRIDGE_KEY`. The inbox key is also stored at `households/{hid}/private/health` { key } (family may read it) and shown under More > Settings > Apple Health (`#settings-health`, `syncHealthCard()`), so an automation can be set up without the console.
- Workflows (Actions tab, the bridge's `FIREBASE_SERVICE_ACCOUNT`; nothing secret typed in or printed): **Household admin** (`tools/household_admin.mjs`: create, add-member, remove-member, new-key, list; it replaced "Seed a user record"; since v78 the app does create, invite and remove itself, so the workflow is for a rescue and for `new-key`), **Households, move the data in** (`tools/migrate_households.mjs`: copy, verify, cleanup, see below), **Publish database rules** (`tools/publish_rules.mjs`: the Firebase Rules REST API with the service account, a ruleset from the file then the `cloud.firestore` release, real or demo project, so the file in git is what is live; firebase-tools' own deploy was tried first and failed because the Admin SDK service account may not ask Service Usage whether Firestore is enabled).
- Migration of the pre-v75 shared layout, done once, with no downtime: (1) `copy` creates the household (or reuses the one that exists), makes members from the old `users/{uid}` records and sets their pointers, copies every collection by the same ids (so re-runs only catch stragglers), links `BRIDGE_KEY` as the inbox key; (2) publish the rules, which carry a TRANSITION block that still answers the old layout to the old `users/{uid}.role`, so an unrefreshed phone keeps working; (3) push the app; (4) `copy` again, `verify`, then `cleanup` with the confirmation text deletes the old top-level data (never `households` or `users`); (5) remove the TRANSITION block from the rules and publish again.
- v76 shipped with `hcol()`/`hdoc()` calling themselves (the bulk substitution had rewritten their own bodies), so a real sign-in opened an app that loaded nothing; fixed in v77 and caught by `pw/signin75.js` in the scratchpad harness, which signs in through the pointer and the members record with every database call recorded. Keep that test in the harness for any change to sign-in or the helpers.
- Tests: `tests/rules/households.test.mjs` (eight cases: family in their own household, another household, strangers signed in or not, read-only writing, the viewer slice, the records that decide access, the size guard, the transition layout), run locally on the emulator before anything went near live data, and by `rules-test.yml` on every push touching the rules.
- Legal, for when other households join: registering with the ICO (about £52 a year), the privacy policy (done in v90), and Apple's rule that healthcare apps be submitted by a legal entity (the Paperwork list in `ROADMAP.md`).

## Sign-up and invites (since v78)
Nobody needs the Firebase console or the Actions tab any more. The app makes the sign-in (Firebase Auth, `createUserWithEmailAndPassword`); the bridge alone makes the records that decide access, after checking the ID token: `POST /household` { name, relation } (a sign-in with no household becomes the family owner of a new one; refused with 409 `already` when it has one; `HOUSEHOLDS_A_DAY` 20 across everyone, counted in `bridge/global`), `POST /invite` { name, role, relation } (family only: a one-use code, `randomCode()`, stored as `households/{hid}/invites/{code}` { name, role, relation, createdBy, createdByName, createdAt, expiresAt (7 days, `INVITE_DAYS`), used, usedBy?, usedAt? } plus the lookup `inviteCodes/{code}` { household, expiresAt }; returns { code, url: the app address with `?invite=code`, expiresAt, householdName }), `GET /invite-info?code=` (no sign-in needed, the code is the secret: { valid, householdName, name, role, from } or the reason it is not), `POST /join` { code, name? } (a sign-in with no household takes the invite's role and relation; the invite is marked used and its lookup removed), `POST /member-remove` { uid } (the owner only, never themselves; the members record, the pointer and their push subscriptions go). `whoIs()` is the shared token check; `memberOf()` the pointer-plus-members read. The demo project refuses all five.
- Sign-in screen: **Create an account** (`#signup-button`, hidden without the bridge) opens `#signup`: first name, The patient / A carer / Family or friend chips (`relationChips()`), email, password (8 or more), an invite code field, a one-line privacy note. Submit makes the sign-in, then `/join` with a code or `/household` without, then `enterApp()`. If the sign-in was made but the bridge call failed, the person lands on the no-household screen, never in limbo. **Forgotten your password?** sends Firebase's reset email to the typed address. `friendlyAuthError()` covers email-already-in-use, weak-password, invalid-email, operation-not-allowed.
- `?invite=CODE` in the address (`readInviteLink()`): kept as `state.pendingInvite`, dropped from the address bar, and a banner on the sign-in screen says who invited whom to which household with what access (from `/invite-info`); Create an account pre-fills the code, and signing in with an existing sign-in that has no household joins automatically (`enterApp()`'s missing branch).
- No-household screen (`#nohousehold`, `showNoHousehold()`): a sign-in with no pointer or no members record stays signed in and sees "x is signed in, but not in a Daybook household yet": first name, relation chips, an invite code box (the button reads Join with this invite once a code is typed, else Create my own Daybook), Sign out. Until v78 that case signed the person out with a message.
- Settings: the **Household** card (`#settings-household`, `syncHouseholdCard()`, family only, not the demo): the household's name, every member with role, relation, owner and you (family may read `members`), Remove with an inline confirm for the owner, and **Invite someone** (`openInviteSheet()`: first name, Family / Read only / Medicines and treatment only with a one-line hint each, A carer / Family or friend / The patient, **Make the invite link**, then the link in a `.keybox` and **Share the invite** through the share sheet or the clipboard, "works once and expires in 7 days").
- `bridgeCall(path, payload)` is the one signed-in call to the bridge (`bridgeExplain()` sits on it). Rules: `inviteCodes` and the top-level `bridge` are denied to everyone (tested). Tested in `pw/v78.js` (sign-up with its own household, email already in use, the invite banner and sign-up with a code, an expired link, the no-household screen with a bad and a good code, forgotten password, the Household card, Remove, the invite sheet; axe on the two new screens and the sheet). Firebase Auth must allow sign-up (Authentication > Settings > User actions, on by default).

## Access
Accounts are data, not code (since v44; households since v75, above). Each sign-in is created under Authentication > Users in the Firebase console, then added to a household with the Household admin workflow, which writes `households/{hid}/members/{uid}` and `users/{uid}.household`. No email address appears in the rules, in `config.js` or anywhere on the site; the repository is public, so that matters. A read that fails or times out (weak signal, the phone waking the app) returns `{ error: true }` after three retries and the person stays signed in, with a "Could not reach Daybook to check your account" message and the Sign in button re-running the check (`enterApp()`, which the auth listener also calls); the last good record is cached on the phone (`localStorage`, `daybook.account.{uid}`) so a weak signal at start-up opens the app at once. `stopData()` resets `recentFrom`, `recentEntries` and `dayEntries` (until v52 a sign-out and sign-in without a reload left the recent-entries feed dead). Two family accounts (Mark, the patient, and Shelley, the carer) have full access. Sign-up and invite links since v78 (above).

### Full read-only access (since v35)
Hayley's account sees everything a family member sees: all six tabs, every report (Notes for the team, Food diary, Documents), every chart, every document, exactly like Mark or Shelley, but cannot create, edit or delete anything anywhere. This is deliberately different from the narrower "viewer" role below, added after that narrower slice turned out not to be enough. Set up by adding them to the household with role `readonly` (Household admin workflow); the rules grant read on every collection in the household (`entries` of every type, `medicines`, `documents` and its `pages`, `profile`, `days`, `exercise`, `meals`, `nutrition`, `recordings`) and write stays family-only on all of them, enforced at the database level.
Signing in as read-only (`state.account.role === "readonly"`, `state.readOnly`) sets `setReadOnlyMode(true)`: a "Read only" pill shows in the topbar (`#readonly-pill`); the bottom nav and landing tab are untouched (all six tabs, lands on Today like anyone else); `startData()` still skips `seedMedicinesIfEmpty()` (a write) but otherwise loads every collection exactly as for a family member. Every add/edit/delete control is hidden throughout the app (`body.is-readonly` in `styles.css`, targeting `.qa`, `#pain-now`, `#meds-manage`, `.med-actions`, `#meds-doc-add`, `#vitals-log`, `#chemo-doc-add`, `#ex-steps-edit`, `#ex-goals-edit`, `#doc-add`, `#doc-edit`, `#doc-paste-summary`, `#doc-save-explanation`, `#doc-delete`, `#more-meals`, `#calls-edit`), while the underlying information stays visible: dose-time chips and the timeline's "..." entry menu render as plain non-interactive text/nothing instead of a button (mirroring the existing `state.viewer` pattern), the exercise goal buttons and Today's check-in rows are disabled in place rather than hidden so their status text stays readable, the document explanation textarea is set `readOnly` rather than removed, and the chemo calendar's day cells stay tappable but `openDaySheet()` renders a plain-text summary (chemo status, mood, "one good thing") with a Close button instead of the checkboxes and Save/Remove buttons a family member gets.

### Narrow read-only viewer
A further account can be added for someone who should see only Mark's medicines, the chemo plan and mood, and the Trends charts, with nothing else visible at all (not even Today, Exercise, More or the reports). Set up by adding them to the household with role `viewer` (none exist today). The rules grant read-only access on `medicines`, `days` (chemo/mood) and `entries` of type `med`, `temp`, `vitals`, `weight`, `sleep` or `drink` only; `food` and `note` entries, `documents`, `exercise`, `profile` and `meals` stay family-only, so this is enforced at the database level, not just hidden in the UI.
Signing in as a viewer (`state.account.role === "viewer"`, `state.viewer`) sets `setViewerMode(true)`: the bottom nav drops to three tabs (Meds, Chemo, Trends), landing on Meds; a "View only" pill shows in the topbar; and every add/edit/delete control on those three tabs is hidden (Log now/Other time/Manage medicines, the Meds tab exemption certificate section, Log vitals, the Reports row, the Chemo cycle section (`#chemo-cycle-section`) and plan documents section, and the calendar days stop being tappable). Today, Exercise, More and the reports are never reachable by a viewer.

## Shared demo (since v56)
A demo people can actually use, as opposed to the in-memory preview below: a SEPARATE Firebase project (free tier, its own database, its own rules in `firestore.demo.rules`: any signed-in user reads and writes everything) with one sign-in, the guest, whose email and password are public by design and live in `config.js` as `window.DAYBOOK_DEMO = { firebase: { ...the demo project's web config }, email, password }`. When that is present the Guest button runs `enterLiveDemo()` instead of the preview: it initialises a second Firebase app (`initializeApp(DEMO.firebase, 'demo')`, `memoryLocalCache`, `inMemoryPersistence` so nothing lingers on the phone), signs in as the guest, points the module-level `auth` and `db` (both `let` since v56; everything reads them at call time, `mainAuth`/`mainDb` keep the real ones) at the demo project, seeds the example data if the demo is empty (`seedDemoIfEmpty()`: the same `buildDemoFixture()` the preview uses plus `SEED_MEDICINES`, written in batches with `demoTs` values turned into Timestamps), then runs the app exactly as for a family member through `enterApp()` (`loadAccount()` returns Guest, family, household `demo`, no `users` record needed). The topbar pill reads "Demo", More says "Guest (shared demo, wiped every night)", the sign-in hint says the same, and a toast warns that others can see what is added and to enter no real details. The bridge and Apple Health are not connected to it. `.github/workflows/reset-demo.yml` runs `tools/reset_demo.mjs` nightly at 03:20 UTC (and by hand): with the `DEMO_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` repository secret (the demo project's service account, never the real one; the script refuses a service account whose project id equals `REAL_PROJECT_ID`) it deletes every document under `households/demo/` (`entries`, `medicines`, `documents` and their `pages`, `recordings` and their `parts`, `profile`, `days`, `exercise`, `meals`, `nutrition`, `bridge`, `pushSubs`, `reminders`, `private`) and, until they are gone, the same top-level collections from before v75, and the app reseeds on the next visit. Switched on 2026-09-28; the first run removed 111 documents. Set-up, once: create the project, turn on Email/Password sign-in and add the guest user, create the Firestore database (London), publish `firestore.demo.rules`, paste the web config and the guest's details into `config.js`, add `DEMO_SERVICE_ACCOUNT` to the repository secrets. Without `window.DAYBOOK_DEMO`, Guest is the preview below.

## Guest preview mode
A "Guest" button on the sign-in screen gives a fully interactive preview of the app with made-up example data, no Firebase account, no sign-in, no Firestore access at all: nothing to create in the console, nothing to hand out. Tapping it sets `state.demo = true` and calls `startDemoData()`, which fills `state` from `buildDemoFixture()` (realistic made-up entries, a chemo calendar, three cycles of check-ins and carer's views, exercise history). Every quick add, edit and delete throughout the file branches on `state.demo` to mutate that in-memory state and re-render, instead of calling Firestore, so the whole app is explorable but nothing is ever written anywhere; it is gone on refresh or on "Leave preview". A `Preview` pill shows in the topbar throughout. Because this path never touches `auth` or `db`, there is nothing to add to `firestore.rules` for it, and no risk of it ever reaching real data.
